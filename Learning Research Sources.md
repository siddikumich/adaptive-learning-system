# Learning research sources

Sources informing [[Learning System]] and [[AI Learning Contract]]. Findings
are evidence to adapt thoughtfully, not guarantees or a reason to optimize
every minute.

## Learning and practice

- [Retrieval practice versus concept mapping — Karpicke & Blunt, *Science* (2011)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/21252317/): retrieval outperformed concept mapping on delayed learning, including inference measures.
- [Distributed practice classroom meta-analysis (2025)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12189222/): distributed practice showed a moderate benefit; timing and effects varied across studies.
- [Interleaving meta-analysis — Brunmair & Richter (2019)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31556629/): moderate overall benefit, with heterogeneous effects; use it for discriminating between confusable problem types after initial acquisition.
- [Active learning in undergraduate STEM — Freeman et al. (2014)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4060654/): active-learning conditions improved exam performance and reduced failure rates across a large meta-analysis.
- [Improving students’ learning with effective learning techniques — Dunlosky et al. (2013)](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/journals/pspi/1529100612453266/): practice testing and distributed practice received high utility ratings; rereading and highlighting were low utility.
- [US Department of Education practice guide: organizing instruction and study](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/PracticeGuide/1): supports spaced retrieval, interleaving, worked examples, and explanatory questions, with recommendation-specific evidence ratings.
- [Near and far transfer in cognitive training — Sala & Gobet (2019)](https://online.ucpress.edu/collabra/article/5/1/18/113004/Near-and-Far-Transfer-in-Cognitive-Training-A): broad meta-analytic review found limited far transfer from cognitive-training programs; practice should target the reasoning contexts that matter.
- [Sleep consistency, quality, duration, and college grades — Okano et al. (2019)](https://www.pnas.org/doi/10.1073/pnas.1817309116): observational evidence connecting sleep measures to course performance; it does not establish a precise causal routine.

## AI-assisted learning

- [ChatGPT-integrated instruction meta-analysis (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1057/s41599-026-07019-z): reported moderate positive learning effects across 35 experimental/quasi-experimental studies, while noting heterogeneity and limited higher-order-thinking evidence.
- [ChatGPT hints in mathematics — controlled study (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pone.0304013): AI hints improved learning compared with no help and were not significantly different from human-tutor help in that setting.
- [ChatGPT as virtual peer in engineering education (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1111/JCAL.13085): a small STEM RCT found benefits from critique/revision-oriented use compared with conventional use.
- [Computer-based feedback meta-analysis — Van der Kleij et al. (2015)](https://doi.org/10.3102/0034654314564881): elaborated feedback outperformed correctness-only feedback, especially for higher-order outcomes.
- [Cornell guidance on AI and academic integrity](https://teaching.cornell.edu/generative-artificial-intelligence/ai-academic-integrity): course policies govern acceptable use; submitted work may require attribution, and students must be able to explain their work.

## Evidence cautions

- AI-learning studies are rapidly evolving and context-dependent. Use the
  protocol because it preserves effortful practice and verification, not because
  any model is inherently reliable.
- Field/preprint findings about AI cognitive offloading and programming learning
  are directionally useful but not treated here as settled evidence.
- For all sources, examine the original study and its population before making a
  strong claim outside its scope.
